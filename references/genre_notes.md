# Genre notes

Short, evidence-based notes per genre pack. A pack says *how* a desire is
paid on its platform; the 5 desires themselves never change.

## Fanqie — rebirth revenge (data/genres/fanqie_rebirth_revenge.yaml)

- Reader: 18–30, fast-satisfaction reading, free + ads, mobile, fragmented time.
- Chapter: 2000–3000 characters, short paragraphs, dialogue-heavy, one small
  payoff per chapter, chapter-end hook mandatory.
- Payoff shape: public humiliation repaid, identity revealed in front of the
  people who looked down on the hero, a resource the hero was denied now
  in their hands.
- Risk: the genre is the most AI-flooded on Fanqie. Differentiation is not
  the premise — it is the payoff actually landing and the foreshadowing
  actually being paid.

## Fanqie — urban (data/genres/fanqie_urban.yaml)

- Reader: same free-reading base, urban / high-martial / brain-hole.
- Payoff shape: a hidden skill used in public, a status reversal in one scene,
  a "brain-hole" rule used cleverly instead of by force.

## Royal Road — LitRPG / progression fantasy (data/genres/royalroad_litrpg.yaml)

- Reader: genre-fluent, review-happy, expects measurable progression
  (levels, skills, stats) and a system with hard rules.
- Chapter: 1500–2500 words, system notifications are prose, not decoration —
  every level-up must change what the hero can do next chapter.
- Payoff shape: identification-upgrade is primary; a level-up that does not
  change the next fight is a broken promise.
- Discovery: Rising Stars rewards strong openings; weak openings are rated
  down fast. The golden 3 gate matters more here than anywhere.
